## **Where this came from**

- **Goal:** the museum should feel like a living, breathing world that goes on without the player. NPCs with visible intentions give the player social cues to read ("excuse me" is the model: they step aside because they were headed somewhere).

- The player should feel a little important, but just a little: others are capable, but only you are allowed to touch the art.

- **The player's motivation:**

    > **Joe (10/9):** the player's motivation is to help others in need. That's the role of a museum: bringing sight to the blind (metaphorically speaking). A sense of belonging stems from that.

- "Master the details" = stop reading the museum and start predicting it. Four layers to master:

    1. **The place** (layout, shortcuts, where things are). Shallowest.

    2. **The rhythm** (when things happen: rushes, breaks, rounds, events). Where a small place gets deep.

    3. **The people.**

        > **Joe (10/9):** problems each one encounters and finding out how to help them.

    4. **The collection** (knowing which piece fits which visitor without reading the placard).

- Joe picked layers 2 and 3 to start: the rhythm and the people.

- Every NPC behavior should have: a visible goal, a consistent rule, and a…

    > **Joe (10/9):** a problem that's solvable by the player

- The world should pay off mastery with recognition, not tokens.

    > **Joe (10/9):** the player is the silent helper. They solve issues before they become issues by doing very specific things. For example: a kid getting lost because he got distracted by the cafe toys while his mom hurries to the theater to catch a showing. The player interferes with the distraction and thus the kid gets to the mom. This is just an example, not gameplay. Museum gameplay shouldn't necessarily rely on events that feel singular, but instead, repeated. So perhaps a group of kids and one gets lost repeatedly because that's more believable.

## **Decisions so far**

- The player is a volunteer. Two modes: **visitor** (off shift) and **volunteer** (on shift). Expectations are only implied when you're clocked in. Nothing is ever demanded; nothing breaks if you don't do it. *(Joe: yes.)*

- The clock is a **hybrid**: the real clock keeps the big things; a per-visit clock runs the busy/quiet rhythm. *(Joe: yes.)*

- **The loop plays once a day** *(Joe 10/11, built)*. The first session of the day plays it. Come back later that day and it carries on where you left it. Once it's over it stays over: what you fixed stays fixed, and what you didn't is still there. The next day starts fresh.

- **Random means random** *(Joe 10/11, built)*:

| Kind | What |
| --- | --- |
| New every session | Things you find: the mug, the cat, dusty and crooked frames, fingerprints on cases, the conservator's case. Anything you cleaned today stays clean all day. |
| Random, kept all day | The loop's tour, the break room mix-up, the janitor's chores, Wednesday's easel spot, who has Sunday off (weighted: the curator only rarely) |
| Random, kept all week | Thursday's trivia seat |
| By the date, on purpose | Who's on shift, the café special and drink of the week, the shop's stock, Bluu's stock, the crowd, the title line, the hourly episode, the weekday events |

- Volunteer clock-in for everyone; the same tokens on or off shift. *(Used in Phase 1.)*

- The rhythm runs in a **12-minute block that starts on arrival**. *(Joe: yes.)*

- The problem chains are the **baseline**; days and unveilings **modify** them. *(Joe: yes.)*

### **Phase 1: the volunteer shift**

**Status:** ✅ built 2026-10-09 (version "2026-11-18 volunteer shift"). Lines to write: Words → Volunteering (marked `[PLACEHOLDER: …]`).

- **Anyone can clock in:** a plain "Volunteer" option at the time clock, no badge needed. Patreon badges keep everything they have now (their name, tally, leaderboard, Employee of the Month).

- **Clock-out line:** one remark from whoever's nearby, based on what you did ("Thanks for getting the plants.") or didn't ("Easy day, huh?").

### **Phase 2: the world does its own chores**

*(Revised 2026-10-09 with Joe's changes.)*

- A **janitor** (new character). His look: suspenders with a matching hat, and a tool belt.

- He always has a **garbage can** with him. He empties the dust from his sweeping into it. *(Changed 10/10: no rolling can. A broom and dustpan while he sweeps, and a trash bag on his trash round that he leaves in the staff room.)*

- He chooses **random chores** to do: sweeping (dust goes into his can), and taking the trash out from each bin in the museum.

- He has a **routine**, which we'll map on the master timeline (see Phase 3).

- He never touches the art, and he doesn't point any of it out to you either. The cases, frames and placards are yours alone, and nobody prompts you about them.

- He **waters the plants**, but only after he's done with his own chores (when that is comes from the master timeline). Any plant you haven't watered by then, he does, and it's crossed off the shift sheet.

- The barista does not water the plant.

### **Phase 3: the rhythm**

The hybrid clock.

- The **real clock** keeps the big things: day / sunset / night, the weekday events, closing.

- A **visit clock** starts when you arrive and loops every 12 minutes (the master timeline below).

    - **Café rush:** a real line at the counter. You take your place and wait, or come back later. Seats fill up.

    - **Breaks** on a schedule you can learn: the shopkeeper steps out at 6–9 ("Back in 15" on the counter), and you wait or come back. Only the shopkeeper breaks in the baseline; Sunday's coffee breaks are their own thing. (Reuses Sunday's coffee-break code.) *(Joe 10/9)*

    - **A tour group** once a visit: the usher leads 4 to 6 visitors wing to wing; they stop at a piece, move on; you step around.

    - **Flow:** more people come in through the front doors early in a visit, and more drift out near the end.

#### **Master timeline**

*(Decided 2026-10-09.)*

- One block is **12 minutes**. It starts when you arrive (the page loads), not when you clock in, so the museum has its rhythm whether you're volunteering or visiting. Stay longer and it loops; things picked at random (like the janitor's chore order) change each time around.

- The real clock sets the mood around it. At night or closing, the block pauses and the night version takes over. Weekday events sit in the block wherever they fit.

- It's for mapping **who does what, when**: the janitor's chores and when he moves on to the plants, staff breaks, the café rush, the tour group, arrivals and departures. Phase 2's janitor routine plugs into it.

- Four beats of 3 minutes each. The "feel" column is only a suggestion until Joe fills it in.

| Tiny | every step, every menu move | barely noticed, but it'd feel dead without it |
| Small | chores, coin finds | a quick puff or sparkle right where it happened |
| Medium | buying, opening a card pack | a short moment, under a second |
| Big | one-off rewards (the token moment) | the screen stops for you |
| Huge | a rare card | the biggest thing in the game |

Everything follows reduced motion (still pictures, no shake), like the token moment.

### **Walking** *(tried in cards 67, rolled back: Joe 10/15, didn't work well)*

- A one-pixel squash each time a foot lands, and a slight stretch as you start moving.
- A small puff of dust at your feet when you start, stop or change direction (not every step).
- Walking into a wall: a one-pixel nudge into it and a tiny squish, instead of stopping dead.
- Followers (curious visitors, the lost kid) do a tiny hop when they catch up to you after falling behind.

### **Cards** *(built, cards 68, except Bluu's fakes)*

- **Buying a card (Joe 10/15):** the screen dims and five card backs slide up from the bottom, one after another. Pick one (highlighted like the binder) and it flips over big. Backs: deep red, black at the edges like a lens vignette, gold trim, the gold GOQ letters.

- **Buying a pack (medium):** the pack drops into the middle of the screen and lands with a squash and a wiggle. A: it tears along the top (a jagged rip line, paper bits). The cards slide out face down into a fan and flip one at a time with a squeeze and pop.
- **A rare card (huge):** the build-up is the fun. A pack with a rare in it wiggles harder. At the rare card it hovers, trembles and glows at the edges, then flips with a white flash and the sunburst in its rarity's color (silver, gold, rainbow for the rarest), a shiny streak sweeping across, confetti, a big RARE! stamp and a little shake.
- **Bluu's fakes:** the glow builds like a rare, then fizzles: the sunburst shrinks away with a sad little puff and a ...FAKE? stamp.

### **Chores (small)**

- Dusting: a dust cloud bursts off the frame, then a glint slides across it.
- Straightening: the frame wobbles past level once and settles.
- Watering: droplets, then the plant bounces up a pixel and stands a little taller.
- Wiping a case: a shine sweeps across the glass.
- Litter in the bin: the lid pops up and drops.

### **Shopping (medium)** *(the wallet counting down is built, cards 68)*

- What you buy hops off the shelf and arcs into a little bag in the corner; the wallet counts down number by number instead of jumping.

### **Menus and text (tiny)**

- The menu cursor bounces a pixel when it moves; the chosen row pops a little.
- The text box pops open with a small overshoot instead of just appearing.

### **Closing up (medium)**

- Each light switch clicks off with a small flash. The last one: the museum dims room by room in a slow wave, then Lights out.

### **Built in cards 70**

- The card flip: the picked card flies to the middle face down, then turns over in 3D (lift, edge-on shading, a shine, a settle). Pack cards fly back into the fan.
- Item get: anything bought that isn't a card pops up big with sparkles and its name, then drops into the bag.
- The café hand-off: the drink is set on the counter (clink, steam), then hops into your hand.
- Red lenses tint the screen red; a fake pair (one lens) darkens the right half. The red lens secret: later.

### **Other**

- Achievements: the toast slides in with an overshoot and the badge spins once.
- A wrong case for a curious visitor: they shake their head (a quick left, right) before saying so.
- Sound, if the game ever gets it: the coin, a card flip, the pack tearing and the wall bump first.
