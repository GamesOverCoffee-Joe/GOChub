## **Where this came from**

- **Goal:** the museum should feel like a living, breathing world that goes on without the player. NPCs with visible intentions give the player social cues to read ("excuse me" is the model: they step aside because they were headed somewhere).

- The player should feel a little important, but just a little: others are capable, but only you are allowed to touch the art.

- **The player's motivation:**

    > **Joe (10/9):** the player's motivation is to help others in need. That's the role of a museum: bringing sight to the blind (metaphorically speaking). A sense of belonging stems from that.

- "Master the details" = stop reading the museum and start predicting it. Four layers to master:

    1. **The place** (layout, shortcuts, where things are). Shallowest.

    2. **The rhythm** (when things happen: rushes, breaks, rounds, events). Where a small place gets deep.

    3. **The people.**

        > **Joe (10/9):** problems each one encounters and finding out how to help them.

    4. **The collection** (knowing which piece fits which visitor without reading the placard).

- Joe picked layers 2 and 3 to start: the rhythm and the people.

- Every NPC behavior should have: a visible goal, a consistent rule, and a…

    > **Joe (10/9):** a problem that's solvable by the player

- The world should pay off mastery with recognition, not tokens.

    > **Joe (10/9):** the player is the silent helper. They solve issues before they become issues by doing very specific things. For example: a kid getting lost because he got distracted by the cafe toys while his mom hurries to the theater to catch a showing. The player interferes with the distraction and thus the kid gets to the mom. This is just an example, not gameplay. Museum gameplay shouldn't necessarily rely on events that feel singular, but instead, repeated. So perhaps a group of kids and one gets lost repeatedly because that's more believable.

## **Decisions so far**

- The player is a volunteer. Two modes: **visitor** (off shift) and **volunteer** (on shift). Expectations are only implied when you're clocked in. Nothing is ever demanded; nothing breaks if you don't do it. *(Joe: yes.)*

- The clock is a **hybrid**: the real clock keeps the big things; a per-visit clock runs the busy/quiet rhythm. *(Joe: yes.)*

- Volunteer clock-in for everyone; the same tokens on or off shift. *(Used in Phase 1.)*

- The rhythm runs in a **12-minute block that starts on arrival**. *(Joe: yes.)*

- The problem chains are the **baseline**; days and unveilings **modify** them. *(Joe: yes.)*

**Status:** ✅ built 2026-10-09 (version "2026-11-18 volunteer shift"). Lines to write: Words → Volunteering (marked `[PLACEHOLDER: …]`).

### **Phase 2: the world does its own chores**

*(Revised 2026-10-09 with Joe's changes.)*

- A **janitor** (new character). His look: suspenders with a matching hat, and a tool belt.

- He always has a **garbage can** with him. He empties the dust from his sweeping into it. *(Changed 10/10: no rolling can. A broom and dustpan while he sweeps, and a trash bag on his trash round that he leaves in the staff room.)*

- He chooses **random chores** to do: sweeping (dust goes into his can), and taking the trash out from each bin in the museum.

- He **waters the plants**, but only after he's done with his own chores (when that is comes from the master timeline). Any plant you haven't watered by then, he does, and it's crossed off the shift sheet.

- The barista does not water the plant.

### **Phase 3: the rhythm**

The hybrid clock.

- The **real clock** keeps the big things: day / sunset / night, the weekday events, closing.

- A **visit clock** starts when you arrive and loops every 12 minutes (the master timeline below).

    - **Café rush:** a real line at the counter. You take your place and wait, or come back later. Seats fill up.

    - **Breaks** on a schedule you can learn: the shopkeeper steps out at 6–9 ("Back in 15" on the counter), and you wait or come back. Only the shopkeeper breaks in the baseline; Sunday's coffee breaks are their own thing. (Reuses Sunday's coffee-break code.) *(Joe 10/9)*

    - **A tour group** once a visit: the usher leads 4 to 6 visitors wing to wing; they stop at a piece, move on; you step around.

    - **Flow:** more people come in through the front doors early in a visit, and more drift out near the end.

#### **Master timeline**

*(Decided 2026-10-09.)*

- One block is **12 minutes**. It starts when you arrive (the page loads), not when you clock in, so the museum has its rhythm whether you're volunteering or visiting. Stay longer and it loops; things picked at random (like the janitor's chore order) change each time around.

- The real clock sets the mood around it. At night or closing, the block pauses and the night version takes over. Weekday events sit in the block wherever they fit.

- It's for mapping **who does what, when**: the janitor's chores and when he moves on to the plants, staff breaks, the café rush, the tour group, arrivals and departures. Phase 2's janitor routine plugs into it.

### **Visitor personalities**

*(Joe's idea 10/10; Claude's plan below, with Joe's answers folded in.)*

Each visitor gets a personality when they come in, and personalities play off each other when they meet. Every personality has the same four attributes, each scored 0 to 4 (0 = never, 4 = as much as possible; walking speed only runs 1 to 3).

| **Personality** | **1. Exhibits** | **2. Sitting** | **3. Speed** | **4. Interacting** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Enthusiast** | 4 | 0 | 2 | 3 |
| **B. Casual** | 3 | 2 | 2 | 3 |
| **C. People Person** | 1 | 0 | 3 | 4 |
| **D. Introvert** | 3 | 4 | 1 | 0 |

What each attribute changes:

- **1. Exhibits:** how often they head for a spot in front of a piece instead of anywhere (today it's 50/50 for everyone), how long they stay there, whether they walk round to read the back of a case, and how often they take a photo.
- **2. Sitting:** multiplies "Visitors who sit down" (Visitors tab): 0 never sits, 4 sits often and for longer.
- **3. Speed:** how brisk their walk is and how long their little pauses between steps are (1 slow, 2 today's pace, 3 brisk).
- **4. Interacting:** how often they start something when they pass another visitor (0 never starts anything). What happens is up to the grid.

**When two meet (the grid).** Rows are whoever starts it, columns whoever they meet, so People Person meeting Introvert can differ from Introvert meeting People Person. No lines are heard or read between people *(Joe 10/10)*: everything is emotes over their heads (the same emote art as photo reactions, so it's all redrawable in Art) and small animations we can actually pull off at this size: a hop, stopping and turning to face each other, stepping back, turning away, walking side by side for a few steps. *(No pointing or nodding: it doesn't read in this format, Joe 10/10.)*

The outcomes a square can pick from (first pass, to tune once we see it):

- **Chat:** both stop, face each other, and trade "…" emotes for a few seconds.
- **Hello:** an emote over one or both as they pass; nobody stops.
- **Hit it off:** both hop, hearts, then walk side by side to a piece.
- **Awkward:** the starter gets a "…", the other a shy emote and steps away.
- **Annoyed:** an annoyed emote and they turn away.
- **Ignore:** nothing happens.

**Keeping it from turning chaotic** (each one a curator setting):

- Only a few meetings at once in the whole museum.
- Each person waits a while before starting another one.
- Never in a doorway, the Lobby Hall, or the café line.
- A meeting has a set length, then everyone carries on.

**Who gets one:** ordinary visitors only. Patreon members don't get a personality *(Joe 10/10)*, and neither do staff, the Janitor, curious visitors while they're asking, the tutorial people, or anyone in a weekday event. The day of the week doesn't change the mix *(Joe 10/10)*.

- A table: one row per personality, with its name, its four numbers, and its share of visitors (e.g. 30 / 35 / 20 / 15%). Add, rename or remove personalities (up to 6).
- The grid: a dropdown in each square for the outcome, and how likely it is.
- The limits above.
- In the Preview tab: **Show personalities** (a small letter over each visitor's head, preview only), and **Make it happen → A meeting** (pick two personalities and they meet beside you).
