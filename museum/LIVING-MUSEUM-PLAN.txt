GOQ MUSEUM: THE LIVING MUSEUM PLAN

A daily rhythm, a volunteer role, and NPCs with their own intentions.

Saved 2026-10-09 so we can pick it up later word for word. Edit freely; mark

==================================================

WHERE THIS CAME FROM (the short version)

==================================================

- Goal: the museum should feel like a living, breathing world that goes on
without the player. NPCs with visible intentions

  give the player social cues to read ("excuse me" is the model: they step aside
because they were headed somewhere).

- The player should feel a little important, but just a little: others are
capable, but only you are allowed to touch the art.

- The player's motivation: **joe note 10/9: the player's motivation is to help
others in need. That's the role of a museum: bringing sight to the blind
(metaphorically speaking). A sense of belonging stems from that.**

  "Master the details" = stop reading the museum and start predicting it. Four
layers to master:

    1. The place (layout, shortcuts, where things are). Shallowest.

    2. The rhythm (when things happen: rushes, breaks, rounds, events). Where a
small place gets deep.

    3. The people **Joe note 10/9: problems each one encounters and finding out
how to help them.**

    4. The collection (knowing which piece fits which visitor without reading
the placard).

- Joe picked layers 2 and 3 to start: the rhythm and the people.

- Every NPC behavior should have: a visible goal, a consistent rule, and am
**Joe note 10/9: a problem that's solvable by the player**

- The world should pay off mastery with recognition, not tokens. **Joe note
10/9: the player is the silent helper. They solve issues before they become
issues by doing very specific things. For example: a kid getting lost because he
got distracted by the cafe toys while his mom hurries to the theater to catch a
showing. The player interferes with the distraction and thus the kid gets to the
mom. This is just an example, not gameplay. Museum gameplay shouldn't
necessarily rely on events that feel singular, but instead, repeated. So perhaps
a group of kids and one gets lost repeatedly because that's more believable.**



Decisions so far:

- The player is a volunteer. Two modes: visitor (off shift) and volunteer (on
shift). Expectations are only implied when

  you're clocked in. Nothing is ever demanded; nothing breaks if you don't do
it. (Joe: yes.)

- The clock is a hybrid: the real clock keeps the big things; a per-visit clock
runs the busy/quiet rhythm. (Joe: yes.)

THE PLAN (four phases, each playable on its own)

==================================================



First, one thing I found that changes Phase 1: clocking in currently needs a
staff badge, and right now those are your

Patreon staff (names, keys). Everyone else can never go "on shift", so for most

yet. Phase 1 opens it up.

PHASE 1: THE VOLUNTEER SHIFT (the foundation)

--------------------------------------------------

STATUS: built 2026-10-09 (version "2026-11-18 volunteer shift"). Decisions used:
volunteer clock-in for everyone; same tokens

on or off shift. Lines to write: Words > Volunteering (marked [PLACEHOLDER:
...]).

- Anyone can clock in: a plain "Volunteer" option at the time clock, no badge
needed. Patreon badges keep everything they

  have now (their name, tally, leaderboard, Employee of the Month).

- On shift: the uniform you already have. The world treats chores as your job.

- Off shift: you can still do chores, but staff react like it's a favor: "Oh,
you didn't have to do that."

- The shift sheet: a handwritten note on the staff corkboard listing today's
actual needs, built from what's really out

  there ("Plants (6)", "Smudges in the Strategy wing", "A crooked frame in the
lobby", "Café bins"). Items get crossed off

  by whoever does them, including coworkers (Phase 2).

- Equipment in its place: a watering can on a hook in the staff room, a duster
in your locker. Just props that say

  "there's a job here."

- Clock-out line: one remark from whoever's nearby, based on what you did
("Thanks for getting the plants.") or didn't

  ("Easy day, huh?").

PHASE 2: THE WORLD DOES ITS OWN CHORES

--------------------------------------------------

(Revised 2026-10-09 with Joe's changes.)

- A janitor (new character). His look: suspenders with a matching hat, and a
tool belt.

- He always has a garbage can with him. He empties the dust from his sweeping
into it.

- He chooses random chores to do: sweeping (dust goes into his can), and taking
the trash out from each bin in the museum.

- He has a routine, which we'll map on the master timeline later (see Phase 3).

- He never touches the art, and he doesn't point any of it out to you either.
The cases, frames and

  placards are yours alone, and nobody prompts you about them.

- He waters the plants, but only after he's done with his own chores (when that
is comes from the master timeline). Any

  plant you haven't watered by then, he does, and it's crossed off the shift
sheet.

- The barista does not water the plant.

you clock in, so the museum has its

  rhythm whether you're volunteering or visiting. Stay longer and it loops;
things picked at random (like the janitor's

  chore order) change each time around.

- The real clock sets the mood around it. At night or closing, the block pauses
and the night version takes over.

  Weekday events sit in the block wherever they fit.

- Four beats of 3 minutes each. The "feel" column is only a suggestion until Joe
fills it in.

  Winding down 9-12 quieter, people leaving

PROBLEMS AND CHAINS: THE BASELINE (Claude 10/9, built from Joe's problems above. Joe: these chains are the baseline.)

Every problem has: a CUE you can read, a WINDOW to prevent it (earlier in the loop), a REPAIR if you missed the window
(never a fail state), and BOTH OUTCOMES play out on their own (the world carries on without you, just better or worse).

  USHER (0-3): needs coffee, too busy with the day's schedules.
    Cue: empty mug on his desk, yawning, glancing toward the café.
    Help: buy a coffee and bring it to him (hand over a drink instead of drinking it).
    Then: at 3-6 he leads the tour. With coffee he's sharp; without it he's foggy (feeds the tour group problem).

  TOUR GROUP (3-6): members complain because the guide doesn't understand the games.
    0-3 they gather in the lobby. 3-6 at a piece they ask a question the usher fumbles.
    Help: step in and explain, only if you've read that placard yourself (the collection, quietly).
    Then: a happy group moves on; a bored one scatters at 6-9, and the kids drift into the gift shop.
    9-12 they leave through the shop. Repeats every loop; one kid gets lost the same way each time (Joe's note:
    repeated, not singular).

  SHOPKEEPER (9-12): messy shelves from the tour kids.
    Cause: a bored tour group (above).
    Prevent: help the tour. Repair: straighten the shelves (a small new chore at the shelving units).
    If neither: the shopkeeper tidies, slowly, with a sigh.

  VISITORS (3-6): trash on the floor because some bins are full.
    Cause: the janitor's random trash route; the bins he hasn't reached yet overflow.
    Help: pick up litter, into a bin with room or the janitor's garbage can.
    If not: he has to sweep it up later (adds to his load, feeds his overtime).

  JANITOR (9-12): working overtime because the plants aren't watered.
    0-9 his random chores (sweeping, trash runs), slowed by litter and crowds. 9-12 the plants.
    Prevent: water the plants earlier (the shift sheet lists them). Then he finishes early and takes his break.
    If not: he's still out at the end of the loop, grumbling, and misses his break.

  BARISTA (6-9): a huge line blocks the walkway because she needed help preparing earlier.
    Cue (0-3): prepping alone; stacks of cups and boxes.
    Help: bring the supply box from storage, or restock the cups (like Monday's boxes).
    Then: 3-6 a prepped café keeps the line moving. If not, at 6-9 the line snakes into the hallway and people squeeze
    around it ("excuse me" happens on its own). 9-12 cleanup; if you helped, a free drink (thanks, not pay).

  CONSERVATOR (9-12): ventures out to the wings, dreaming about the day his own game gets put up.
    0-9 in storage, crates for the next unveiling. 9-12 standing in front of an empty wall spot.
    Help: talk to him. Not a fix: a moment, which can grow over many visits (his game getting featured). The usher's
    Sunday gossip already sets it up.

  STAFF ON BREAK (9-12): someone left, accidentally taking someone else's personal item.
    6-9 two members on break; headphones or a lunchbox on the table.
    9-12 one leaves with the wrong thing; the other asks "has anyone seen my...?"
    Help: catch the first one on the way out (the lobby) and bring it back. Owners and items rotate (Patreon members).

  THE CHAINS
    Bins (janitor's route) -> litter (visitors) -> more sweeping -> janitor overtime <- unwatered plants
    Usher's coffee -> how good the tour is -> bored kids -> messy shop
    Barista's prep -> the line -> a blocked walkway

  Not every problem every loop: 3 or 4 of them per loop, chosen by the day, so loops differ and the patterns are
  learned over several visits.
  Idea: a helper's notebook (like Majora's Mask's Bombers' Notebook): people you've helped and what you've noticed
  about their routines, maybe on the back of the shift sheet.


DAY MODIFIERS (Joe 10/9: the chains are the baseline; the day of the week, or an unveiling, bends them, so each day
has a different shape. Claude's first pass below, built on what each day already has.)

  A modifier does one of four things to a chain: makes a link STRONGER (more likely, bigger), WEAKER, MOVES it to a
  different beat or place, or ADDS a new link.

  MONDAY (the misplaced boxes)
    - Delivery day: more boxes. The barista's supply box is one of them (stronger: her prep chain).
    - The janitor has boxes to break down (adds a chore: overtime more likely).
    - The conservator is busy unpacking crates, so he doesn't come out to the wings.

  TUESDAY (the lost kid)
    - The tour group is a school group: more kids (stronger: bored kids -> messy shop).
    - The lost kid and the tour group's lost kid can be the same story on Tuesdays.

  WEDNESDAY (the artist)
    - A crowd gathers around the artist's easel: litter collects there (moves the litter to one wing).
    - The tour group stops at the easel instead of a piece (weaker: the usher's fumble).

  THURSDAY (trivia night)
    - The café is busiest late: the line moves from 6-9 to 9-12, and the barista has trivia to set up too (stronger).

  FRIDAY (the episode in the Screening Nook)
    - At a set time the crowd flows to the nook: the wings empty out, and the nook gets the litter (moves).
    - The usher is scheduling the showing (stronger: he needs that coffee).

  SATURDAY (Bluu's stall)
    - A crowd at the stall in the lobby: a blocked walkway in the lobby too (adds).
    - Bluu's customers leave packaging behind: bins fill faster (stronger: litter, janitor).
    - The shopkeeper is grumpy about the competition (cue only).

  SUNDAY (half-price café, coffee breaks with staff)
    - Half price means a much bigger café line (stronger).
    - Staff take turns on their coffee break: a counter stands empty for a while (adds a "come back later").

  UNVEILING DAY (any day with an unveiling)
    - The ceremony draws everyone to one wing at a set time: other areas empty out.
    - The usher runs the ceremony instead of a tour (the tour chain is off).
    - Right after: a rush on the gift (stronger: messy shop) and on the drink of the week (stronger: the line).

  Every day: how busy the museum is (light / medium / heavy, already in the game) makes the lines, litter and crowds
  bigger or smaller.
