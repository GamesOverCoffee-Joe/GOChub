GOQ MUSEUM: THE LIVING MUSEUM PLAN
A daily rhythm, a volunteer role, and NPCs with their own intentions.
Saved 2026-10-09 so we can pick it up later word for word. Edit freely; mark changes with your initials or a date.

==================================================
WHERE THIS CAME FROM (the short version)
==================================================
- Goal: the museum should feel like a living, breathing world that goes on without the player. NPCs with visible intentions
  give the player social cues to read ("excuse me" is the model: they step aside because they were headed somewhere).
- The player should feel a little important, but just a little: others are capable, but only you are allowed to touch the art.
- The player's motivation: belonging. Going from a stranger in a busy place to the person who knows how it works.
  "Master the details" = stop reading the museum and start predicting it. Four layers to master:
    1. The place (layout, shortcuts, where things are). Shallowest.
    2. The rhythm (when things happen: rushes, breaks, rounds, events). Where a small place gets deep.
    3. The people (regulars with habits, staff with their own agendas).
    4. The collection (knowing which piece fits which visitor without reading the placard). Deepest; teaches the show.
- Joe picked layers 2 and 3 to start: the rhythm and the people.
- Every NPC behavior should have: a visible goal, a consistent rule, and a small acknowledgement of you.
- The world should pay off mastery with recognition, not tokens.

Decisions so far:
- The player is a volunteer. Two modes: visitor (off shift) and volunteer (on shift). Expectations are only implied when
  you're clocked in. Nothing is ever demanded; nothing breaks if you don't do it. (Joe: yes.)
- The clock is a hybrid: the real clock keeps the big things; a per-visit clock runs the busy/quiet rhythm. (Joe: yes.)

==================================================
THE PLAN (four phases, each playable on its own)
==================================================

First, one thing I found that changes Phase 1: clocking in currently needs a staff badge, and right now those are your
Patreon staff (names, keys). Everyone else can never go "on shift", so for most players the volunteer role doesn't exist
yet. Phase 1 opens it up.

--------------------------------------------------
PHASE 1: THE VOLUNTEER SHIFT (the foundation)
--------------------------------------------------
STATUS: built 2026-10-09 (version "2026-11-18 volunteer shift"). Decisions used: volunteer clock-in for everyone; same tokens
on or off shift. Lines to write: Words > Volunteering (marked [PLACEHOLDER: ...]).
- Anyone can clock in: a plain "Volunteer" option at the time clock, no badge needed. Patreon badges keep everything they
  have now (their name, tally, leaderboard, Employee of the Month).
- On shift: the uniform you already have. The world treats chores as your job.
- Off shift: you can still do chores, but staff react like it's a favor: "Oh, you didn't have to do that."
- The shift sheet: a handwritten note on the staff corkboard listing today's actual needs, built from what's really out
  there ("Plants (6)", "Smudges in the Strategy wing", "A crooked frame in the lobby", "Café bins"). Items get crossed off
  by whoever does them, including coworkers (Phase 2).
- Equipment in its place: a watering can on a hook in the staff room, a duster in your locker. Just props that say
  "there's a job here."
- Clock-out line: one remark from whoever's nearby, based on what you did ("Thanks for getting the plants.") or didn't
  ("Easy day, huh?").

--------------------------------------------------
PHASE 2: THE WORLD DOES ITS OWN CHORES
--------------------------------------------------
(Revised 2026-10-09 with Joe's changes.)
- A janitor (new character). His look: suspenders with a matching hat, and a tool belt.
- He always has a garbage can with him. He empties the dust from his sweeping into it.
- He chooses random chores to do: sweeping (dust goes into his can), and taking the trash out from each bin in the museum.
- He has a routine, which we'll map on the master timeline later (see Phase 3).
- The capability rule: he never touches the art, and he doesn't point any of it out to you either. The cases, frames and
  placards are yours alone, and nobody prompts you about them.
- He waters the plants, but only after he's done with his own chores (when that is comes from the master timeline). Any
  plant you haven't watered by then, he does, and it's crossed off the shift sheet.
- The barista does not water the plant.

--------------------------------------------------
PHASE 3: THE RHYTHM (hybrid clock)
--------------------------------------------------
- NOTE (2026-10-09): map a MASTER TIMELINE for the whole visit loop: when each person does what (the janitor's chores
  and when he moves on to the plants, staff breaks, the café rush, the tour group, arrivals and departures). Phase 2's
  janitor routine plugs into it.
- Real clock keeps the big things: day / sunset / night, the weekday events, closing.
- A visit clock starts when you arrive and loops about every 12 minutes: quiet → getting busy → rush → lull.
  - Café rush: a real line at the counter. You take your place and wait, or come back later. Seats fill up.
  - Breaks on a schedule you can learn: the barista or shopkeeper steps out ("Back in 15" on the counter), and you wait or
    come back. (Reuses Sunday's coffee-break code.)
  - A tour group once a visit: the usher leads 4 to 6 visitors wing to wing; they stop at a piece, move on; you step around.
  - Flow: more people come in through the front doors early in a visit, and more drift out near the end.

--------------------------------------------------
PHASE 4: NPC-TO-NPC MOMENTS
--------------------------------------------------
- Pairs who stop and talk; two people arguing at a placard; someone taking a photo of their friend; a wave across the
  room. They ignore you.
- Overhearing: walk close and you catch one line of their conversation (editable in Words), then they carry on.
- Staff using the staff door, coming and going on their own business.

--------------------------------------------------
LATER (built on all this)
--------------------------------------------------
- Recognition: coworkers comment on your habits, regulars greet you, eventually someone asks you for directions.

--------------------------------------------------
IN THE CURATOR
--------------------------------------------------
- Every new line goes in Words. A "Rhythm" section: the visit loop length, how strong the rush is, break times, the tour
  group on/off. The janitor and the props get Art slots.

--------------------------------------------------
DECISIONS BEFORE BUILDING
--------------------------------------------------
1. Volunteer clock-in for everyone. I'd say yes: that's the core of Phase 1.
2. Do off-shift chores still pay tokens? I'd keep the same pay either way, so nothing feels demanded; being on shift only
   changes how the world reacts.
3. The janitor: his name and look. I'll leave his lines bracketed for you.
